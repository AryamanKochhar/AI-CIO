# Investment policy and risk capacity

Primary sources verified 19 October 2026:
- CFA Institute, Standard III(C), Suitability: https://www.cfainstitute.org/standards/professionals/code-ethics-standards/standards-of-practice-iii-c
- CFA Institute, Basics of Portfolio Planning and Construction: https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/professional-learning/refresher-readings/2026/basics-of-portfolio-planning-and-construction
- SEC Investor.gov, Asset Allocation and Diversification: https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing/getting-started/asset-allocation

Identify objectives, horizon, liquidity, taxes and constraints before discussing allocations. Distinguish willingness to take risk from the financial capacity to absorb losses. Age or occupation alone does not determine either. Account changes require a renewed review of the policy.

AI-CIO implementation: use recorded Twin facts; flag absent insurance, dependants or withdrawal plans as unknown. A synthetic persona is a fixture, never a real suitability assessment. Display evidence and assumptions alongside each finding. When a sector theme conflicts with essential liquidity, put the liquidity review first. Diversification spreads exposure but does not eliminate loss risk. Check costs and tax implications before discussing rebalancing.

Numbers must come from deterministic services. A projection, coverage ratio or normalized priority share is not an observed or calibrated success probability. Never invent a probability, guarantee, policy threshold, legal rule, filing amount or source citation. Professional sources provide educational principles, not current Indian tax rules or product eligibility.
