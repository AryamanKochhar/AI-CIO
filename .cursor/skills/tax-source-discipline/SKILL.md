# Tax source and applicability discipline

Primary source: tax_act_transition, Income Tax Department, verified 20 October 2026. The source manifest is contextual evidence, not an implemented rate table. Never introduce tax rates from model memory.

## Establish applicability first
Require the relevant income/tax period, disposal/acquisition dates, asset classification, residency/regime where relevant, transaction history, cost basis and the engine rule version. A display date or upload date is not necessarily a disposal date. A holding valuation alone cannot establish realized gains.

## Handle the 2026 transition
Official transition FAQs distinguish income periods beginning before 1 April 2026 from Tax Year 2026-27 onward under the Income-tax Act, 2025. Do not relabel every historical computation using the current Act or infer a rate change merely from renumbered sections. Earlier-period treatment needs its applicable rule version.

## Use calculations only within coverage
Cite engine amounts as estimates with their stated coverage and as-of period. Missing dates or cost basis mean not evaluated for affected calculations, rather than zero tax. TDS, liability, realized gain and cash proceeds are different concepts. An FD interest statement does not by itself establish the taxpayer's final liability.

## Review opportunities rather than guarantee savings
A potential loss/gain harvest requires complete relevant lots, applicable utilization rules, known costs, suitability and timing. Never invent an exemption, deadline, set-off, carry-forward permission or filing amount. Ask the user to verify the missing source record or seek professional confirmation before consequential action. Book frameworks may explain trade-offs; official versioned rules govern legal assertions.
