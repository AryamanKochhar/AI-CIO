# The Psychology of Money

Source: `docs/books/The_Psychology_of_Money.pdf` (Morgan Housel).

Core thesis: doing well with money depends more on **behaviour** than on intelligence or formal finance knowledge. Use for tone, education, and behavioural framing in AI-CIO — **not** as market prediction or product pitches.

## When to use

- Onboarding / psychometric / goal-setting copy
- Explaining why two smart users disagree about risk
- Framing savings, compounding, and “enough”
- Softening market-observation UI without becoming advice
- Behavioural context alongside NISM risk-profiling

## Hard rules for AI-CIO

1. **Behaviour before brilliance** — prefer sustainable habits (save, diversify concepts, stay invested education) over “smart trades”.
2. **Reasonable > rational** — if a theoretically optimal allocation will be abandoned in a drawdown, it is a bad product default.
3. **Room for error** — incomplete Twin data → say unknown; build buffers into projections; never precision-wash guesses.
4. **Luck & risk symmetry** — when praising an outcome, acknowledge risk; when criticizing, acknowledge luck.
5. **Enough** — help users define goals; do not fuel endless “beat the market” framing.
6. **Volatility as fee** — explain drawdowns as the cost of equity-like returns, not as bugs to panic-fix.
7. **No one’s crazy** — personalize explanations using *their* horizon and history, not a universal scold.
8. Still obey product law: **observation, not recommendation**; no guaranteed outcomes.

## Copy patterns

**Good:** “Markets charge a fee in the form of ups and downs. Staying aligned with your horizon matters more than reacting to one week.”

**Bad:** “Smart investors buy the dip now — don’t be crazy and sell.”

**Good:** “Your plan only works if you can stick with it. Here is a simpler allocation band to review.”

**Bad:** “The optimal mean-variance portfolio is exactly 63.2% equity.”

## Do NOT

- Use Housel anecdotes as evidence that a specific stock/fund will win
- Shame users for past behaviour
- Equate “reasonable” with “do nothing about real concentration risk”
- Override NISM/SEBI suitability and disclosure requirements
