# Money behaviour and plan durability

Book context: Morgan Housel, The Psychology of Money (2020), existing user-provided source under docs/books. The following is an original implementation framework; book stories do not validate a personal prediction. Source cfa_suitability separately supports client-context review, not endorsement of the book.

## Separate statement, observation and interpretation
A questionnaire records declared preferences. An observed pattern needs actual dated transactions or comparable snapshots. Do not infer panic, overconfidence or a disorder from a persona, one sale, an absent record or a low balance. Use neutral descriptions and state insufficient history when appropriate.

## Respect scoring polarity
Read the profile's higher_means/lower_means labels. A high score for a bias-named dimension may indicate less of that bias. Never invert the meaning because the field name contains loss aversion or anchoring.

## Turn behaviour into useful review
If stated willingness and observed concentration differ, name the supported difference and ask which reflects the user's current intention. Discuss plan simplicity, liquidity flexibility and tolerable uncertainty as review dimensions. Do not shame the user or prescribe doing nothing when genuine concentration or cash-flow concerns are present.

Book lenses include room for error, distinguishing luck from skill, defining enough and choosing sustainable habits. These are educational framing, not evidence of expected returns. Use facts first, then a tentative explanation and a specific action such as confirming a constraint or comparing a goal timeline. Avoid numerical psychological precision, fabricated response times, promises of superior outcomes and ticker instructions.
