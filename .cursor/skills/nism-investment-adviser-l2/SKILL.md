# Integrated financial planning

Educational source: existing NISM Series X-B workbook under docs/books; public curriculum: nism_l2_curriculum. Professional framing: cfa_suitability and sebi_adviser. These resources do not authorize financial execution or personalized tax/legal opinions.

## Compare the complete situation
Assess risk willingness separately from loss-bearing capacity and goal need. A conflict between them is a finding to review, not permission to increase risk automatically. Consider liquidity, debt obligations, goal dates, costs and known restrictions alongside the portfolio.

## Use a trade-off matrix
For each supported option, identify the affected goal, liquidity effect, exposure change, known costs, possible tax consequences and reversibility. Do not invent an amount to complete a row. Reducing concentration can realize tax; waiting can preserve exposure risk. Increasing contributions can reduce current cash flexibility. Describe both sides using supplied engine outputs.

## Make action conditional and inspectable
A concrete next step may be confirming a goal deadline, checking cost basis, reviewing bank-level deposits or comparing policy allocation with current exposure. It must explain what the user will learn. Where an essential input is absent, request that input instead of declaring a strategy suitable.

Product comparisons require equivalent horizon, liquidity, risk, cost and tax assumptions. Distinguish a gross rate from an after-tax outcome. Estate, insurance and pension mechanisms can be discussed as educational categories, but current eligibility and drafting questions need official/professional confirmation. No product ranking, return promise, clinical label or autonomous transaction follows from this skill.
