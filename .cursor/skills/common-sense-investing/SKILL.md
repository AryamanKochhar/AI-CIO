# Diversification, cost and discipline

Book context: John C. Bogle, The Little Book of Common Sense Investing, updated edition, Wiley, 2017, ISBN 9781119404507. The existing publisher link remains https://www.wiley.com/en-us/the-little-book-of-common-sense-investing-the-only-way-to-guarantee-your-fair-share-of-stock-market-returns-updated-and-revised-p-9781119404507 . The complete book is not bundled or claimed to have been read. Source sebi_fund_costs provides separate current public education on plan costs.

## Compare like with like
A low-cost diversified category can be a comparison baseline, not an automatic recommendation. Inspect disclosed expense ratio, transaction costs, exit charges, tracking characteristics and tax consequences where available. Unknown costs stay unknown. Do not invent a savings amount or subtract expenses twice from a return already net of fees.

## Diversification is exposure, not product count
Use actual holding/issuer/sector weights and available look-through. Several funds can overlap. An AMC label is not the same as underlying company exposure. Report unclassified exposure explicitly; no universal holding count proves adequate diversification.

## Rebalancing is a policy review
Compare recorded allocation with the user's stated policy or engine-labelled review thresholds. Consider reviewing future contributions before discussing a sale, and explain tax, transaction cost, liquidity and tracking trade-offs. Neither route is universally best. Do not invent target bands, a schedule or a safe-harbour threshold.

Explain the observed drift, why it matters for a known constraint, supported alternatives and what the user should inspect next. Short-term momentum does not replace a long-term policy. Never repeat guarantee wording from a book title as an application promise or claim that low fees eliminate market loss.
