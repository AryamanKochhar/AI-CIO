# Cash budgets and compounding costs

Sources: sec_fee_drag and sebi_investment_constraints in knowledge/finance_sources.json. US education explains cost mechanics, not Indian disclosure or tax law.

## Separate capacity from commitment
Recorded monthly surplus = income minus recorded outgoings, after normalizing cadence. Check whether EMI and existing contributions are included. Available surplus is not consent to invest that amount. Request the person's monthly investment budget, current commitments, reserve needs and income stability. If that budget is absent, describe surplus-funded paths as illustrative and unconfirmed. A psychometric answer cannot supply a rupee budget.

## Follow the fee cashflow
A fee leaves less capital available for later growth. In a toy model with constant gross return r and a proportional end-year fee f, W[n] = P * ((1 + r) * (1 - f))^n. State the charging convention; it differs from simply subtracting f from r. Costs reduce the capital left to compound. Do not deduct an expense ratio again from a return already net of it.

## Evaluate a change rather than only a cheap product
Request actual product identity, recurring charges, transaction costs, exit load, taxes and comparable exposure. A cheaper product can have different liquidity, tracking or risk. Fee comparisons hold other assumptions equal and do not prove future outperformance or justify switching. Unknown fees cannot support a savings estimate.
